Business understanding

Problem and research questions

LLM API prices differ by orders of magnitude and change often. A fixed budget makes model choice hard.

RQ1 · Tiers

What price/performance tiers exist?

H1: at least 3 tiers, including one with high price but no matching benchmark advantage.

Result Supported

RQ2 · Classification

Can the tier be predicted without price or score?

H2: a classifier beats the majority baseline (H2a), and maker or open/closed rank in the top 3 features (H2b).

Result Partial

RQ3 · Selection

Does a tier-based rule pick better models?

H3: in a 2025 → 2026 backtest, ranking by score ÷ price beats "cheapest" and "top benchmark".

Result Not supported

How the four numbers are measured

[N]: distinct text-model names in the clean catalog. [M]: distinct providers in it. [X]: tiered models split into 10 performance deciles; largest max ÷ min blended price inside one decile. [Y]: share of the … models in the weekly price history (Jan 2025 → Sep 2026) whose input or output price changed at least once.

Related work and method

Earlier work tracked LLM price trends and quality-adjusted price indexes (NBER w34608; The Price of Intelligence, 2026) or routed requests to cheaper models (FrugalGPT). None split the market into tiers or tested a budget rule with a backtest.

Process: CRISP-DM. Tools: Python, pandas, scikit-learn, XGBoost, SHAP. Fixed seeds everywhere.

Reading the results

  • Shuffled labels never beat the real model, so it is not guessing.
  • 70% of predictions are within one price or score band step (chance: 50%).
  • Release date ranks 1st. Newer models score higher. This is partly real progress and partly era bias in the percentile score.
  • Exploratory, after the verdict: a size word in the model name (mini/flash vs pro/max) lifts macro F1 from … to ….
H2 decision rules

H2a: best model's macro F1 above the majority baseline and permutation p < 0.05. H2b: lab or open_weights in the top 3 of both SHAP and permutation importance. open_weights is 3rd (SHAP) and 4th (permutation); lab is 5th and 8th → partial.

Modeling & evaluation · RQ3

RQ3 · Model selection backtest H3 not supported

Ranking by score ÷ price picks the same model as "cheapest" for coding. It never beats "top benchmark" on the mean.

Decision algorithm. Input: task, requirements, monthly token usage, budget.

  1. Filter by task capability (coding, reasoning, vision).
  2. Filter by requirements (open-weight, min context, providers, tools + JSON).
  3. Filter by budget: expected tokens × price ≤ monthly budget.
  4. Rank by value = task score ÷ price.
  5. Return the top 3 with their tier.

Backtest: decide with data up to 2025-12-29, then score the pick at 2026-09-28. A pick scores 0 if it went over budget or was no longer sold.

Limitations

  • 144 tiered models is a small sample. The smallest tier has 6.
  • Many composite scores rest on 1–3 benchmarks.
  • The 2025-12 backtest covers coding only. Reasoning and vision benchmarks cover 2026 models only.
